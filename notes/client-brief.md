# Client brief: Kenyon Model United Nations

## Who runs the site
The club's officers run it: the President and the Vice President. These two are the sole admins. They are moderately comfortable with computers, perhaps being college students speaks for itself. Club members write the recaps of each trip, and editors with their own logins can publish their own posts, while only the admins manage users and everything else.

## Who reads it
The Kenyon community. They come to remain updated on how the Model United Nations competitions go for the team: where the team traveled, how the competition was, whether it was competitive, whether they explored the new place, whether they tried any new foods. Ultimately it's just detailing their travels. The club travels once or twice a semester, most recently to Chicago, so posts are infrequent and each one matters.

## What is broken now
The club doesn't have an official site. It uses Instagram to publicize any club activities, so recaps get buried in a feed and nobody can look back at a past trip.

## What would make them say "this is better"
An active website with accessible blogs that include pictures and anecdotes would be a much better alternative to Instagram. If possible, they'd also like a comment section, but that's a nice-to-have, not a requirement.

## What must not happen
Nobody except approved editors can edit the blogs. An editor must never be able to create users, change roles, or edit another person's recap.
